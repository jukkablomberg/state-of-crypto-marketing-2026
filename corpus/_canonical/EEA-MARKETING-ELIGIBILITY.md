question a CMO actually asks: *can this company legally acquire customers in the EEA, and where can it advertise?*

## THE MARKETING IMPLICATION

**Two firms in twenty-seven lost the right to acquire EEA customers — and one of them is the largest exchange on
earth.** Binance executed a complete EU acquisition shutdown on 01/07/2026: no new sign-ups, no new deposits, no new
orders, consultation and withdrawal only. Under ESMA's own wind-down ordering the **first** obligation is to cease
marketing and solicitation — which makes **paid media going dark the leading public indicator of licence failure,
ahead of any product change.** Euro pairs are ~1% of Binance's global spot book, so this is not a revenue story. It
is the cleanest demonstration in the corpus that a decision taken far above the marketing function lands **first**
on the marketing function.

**The moat is real and narrower than the licence count suggests.** Within days of 1 July, six licensed firms were
running dated, quantified, EEA-geofenced capture campaigns at the stranded users: OKX 8% (€20k cap, €50M pool),
Coinbase 5% before 13 July, Kraken a €1M draw, Bitpanda €25 BTC + 3% cashback, Bitvavo and Gate up to 10%. Four are
tracked Tier-1s licensed by four different NCAs — a jurisdiction-diverse land-grab, not one friendly regulator's
firms. **The licence became the offer**: Kraken's landing page ran *"Most exchanges aren't licensed after July 1.
Kraken is."* But **holding a licence is not using one — Gemini holds 29 states and withdrew from the EU in February
2026.** A moat you do not defend is a filing.

**The Google gate is the part that actually changes a media plan.** It converts MiCA status from a compliance
attribute into an **inventory-eligibility flag**: since 23/04/2025 across EU-27 and since August 2026 across the
full EEA, advertising a crypto exchange *or software wallet* requires a CASP authorisation plus Google
certification. Remedy is account suspension on ≥7 days' notice. On 01/07/2026 Google stopped accepting AMF DASP
registration as an advertising credential and published the change the same day. **For a media buyer the register
row is now upstream of the campaign brief: no CASP entry, no paid search, no YouTube, no Display, across thirty
states at once.** Three consequences: the licence becomes the highest-value claim in EU crypto marketing;
unauthorised firms are pushed into creators, Telegram, X and affiliates — precisely the surface the FCA had to name
nine platforms to reach in the HTX claim; and **the gate gates the advertiser, not the ad.** An authorised CASP
running a materially misleading ad passes without friction. ⚠ Two limits: Google is not a regulator, its remedy is
contractual; and **whether it enforces its own rule is entirely unmeasured.**

**The segmentation nobody talks about — one licence name, two populations.** Of 324 rows carrying a passport list:
**132 (40.7%) authorised for 29–30 states**, 17 more for 26–28 → **149 (46.0%) at 26–30**, against **123 (38.0%)
authorised for exactly one state.** Median 10, mean 15.0 — **and the mean describes nobody**: only 69 of 324 sit
between 2 and 28. Roughly four in ten licensed CASPs can run a continental campaign; roughly four in ten can run
only a domestic one.

**The tracked cohort is drawn almost entirely from the top mode — 11 of 13 entities at 26–30.** That is a scope
disclosure this report owes its reader before it is a finding.

🔴 **And the newest entrants are pulling the other way.** Of 35 firms authorised on or after 01/07/2026, **23
(65.7%) took a single-market authorisation**, against 33.8% of the 293 pre-deadline firms. **All fourteen German
post-deadline entrants — twelve of them cooperative or regional retail banks — took Germany alone. Not one took a
passport.** The post-deadline entrant to European crypto services is not a crypto-native firm building an EU
marketing function; it is a **Volksbank adding a product line for customers it already has**, with no cross-border
acquisition ambition at all. *(Limit attached: a 35-row window against a years-deep back-catalogue; the register
records status at capture, not intent.)*

## GAPS — what the register cannot tell a marketer

1. **Where anyone publishes.** `ae_website_platform` — the one column about the promotional surface — is populated
   in **47 of 329 rows**, and after removing column-bleed corruption **exactly two firms** record a trading surface
   distinct from their corporate website.
2. **Whether a licence is being used.** No field for market activity, media spend or acquisition motion.
3. **Whether the Google gate is enforced**, and whether a non-custodial wallet or hardware-wallet maker is treated
   as a gated advertiser. Google's categories name them; MiCA's perimeter does not cover them. **Untested.**
4. **Six other distribution surfaces.** Meta, X, TikTok, App Store, Google Play, Reddit, LinkedIn — **never swept.**
   One platform of at least seven has been read at source. A media plan is not a Google media plan.
5. **Anything about conduct.** `ae_reason` is empty in 166 of 167 rows; 165 of 167 are Italian; Germany, which has
   licensed more firms than anyone (73 of 324), appears zero times. **This is one authority's reporting practice,
   not a measure of European enforcement.**
6. **KOL and influencer disclosure — unfillable at every firm.** Not one creator post or paid-partnership label
   captured for any tracked firm, including the named creator partners in Bitpanda's own campaign.
7. **Entities are not brands.** Kraken: two Irish entities, 30 and 2 states. Bitpanda: three entities, three NCAs,
   one blank passport cell. Name-keyed search fails silently — "Bitstamp by Robinhood" and Aave/"Push" are live
   examples. **Never aggregate per-entity numbers to a firm.**
8. **A snapshot is not a feed.** One row is forward-dated 28/08/2026 inside a 17/08/2026 capture — the defect is in
   the source. Do not restate these numbers against a later register state.
