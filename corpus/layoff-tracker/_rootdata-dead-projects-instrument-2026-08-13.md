## The finding: the number the corpus has been carrying is a range, and the source says so itself

The 08-10 denominator note recorded CoinDesk's *"over 100 crypto projects"* as the universe. **The captured feature is materially more careful than that, and its care is the corpus-relevant part:**

> "The scale depends on how you count, so here is the honest range. Independent lists compiled from public shutdown statements put the fully verified tally at more than 100 projects between January and late July. The Web3 data platform RootData, which also counts bankruptcy filings and projects whose websites have gone dark for extended periods, puts the figure at roughly **95-110 depending on the snapshot date** — around **70 by the end of June**, **99-101 across most early-August verified snapshots**, and approximately **110 by RootData's most recent late-July count**."

And, in the same piece:

> "Not every entry on RootData's list is a collapse; some are orderly wind-downs, pivots, or single-product sunsets, and the list treats a bankruptcy filing and a community-voted shutdown as equivalent data points."

**Three things follow, and Phase 2 must carry all three.**

1. **"100+" is a snapshot, not a count.** The same source gives ~70 (end of June), 99–101 (early-August verified), ~110 (late-July RootData). **A late-July snapshot is larger than an early-August one** — which is only possible if the list is being revised downward on verification. **Any single figure printed without its snapshot date is wrong by construction.**
2. **The universe is heterogeneous in a way that defeats the recall framing.** RootData counts a Chapter 11 filing, a community-voted sunset, a single-product deprecation and a dark website as one unit each. **The corpus's 24 tracker rows are workforce-contraction events.** These are not the same object, and — restating the 08-10 rule in stronger form — **they must not be divided by one another.** The 08-10 note said RootData's 100+ and CryptoJobsList's 54 are different objects; **today's capture shows RootData's list is not even internally one object.**
3. **The instrument is a project-mortality list, not a layoff list.** It was named on 08-10 as the object that "sizes the universe class 5 samples." **On capture, it does not.** It sizes a *different* universe. **The class-5 denominator remains unmeasured, and the corpus should stop expecting this instrument to supply it.**

## Structural drivers, captured verbatim-in-substance — relevant to Theme 5, not to the tracker

The piece attributes the 2026 wave to a **funding wall** rather than the hack crisis that drove H1: crypto startups raised roughly **$4bn across 355 deals in Q1 2026, a ~50% quarter-on-quarter drop in capital** (attributed to Galaxy Research, `galaxy.com/insights/research/crypto-blockchain-vc-venture-capital-startups-fundraising-q1-2026` — **NOT FETCHED**); about **eight crypto funds raising ~$1.1bn in Q1**; token-denominated treasuries compressing "from years of runway to months"; TRM Labs attributing the majority of 2026 hack losses to state-linked operations (`trmlabs.com/resources/blog/h1-2026-crypto-hacks-reach-record-high-as-losses-fall-below-usd-1-billion` — **NOT FETCHED**).

**Why this matters to a marketing report and not just a market one:** the captured source's own summary of the survivors is *"proven real revenue rather than token-subsidized growth."* **That is the same fault line the layoff tracker's nine consecutive non-AI rationales describe from the other side.** Watch (h′) — "layoff rationale correlates with firm type" — **stays rejected**, but this capture is the first time an external source has named the correlate the corpus keeps almost-seeing: **funding structure, not firm type, and not AI.** Stated as a hypothesis with a named source, **not as a corpus finding.**
