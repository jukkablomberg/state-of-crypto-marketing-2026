## Why this file exists, and what it corrects

**Bitpanda has been recorded as producing zero in-window public marketing signal on four consecutive runs** (07-20, 07-23, 07-27 AM, 07-27 PM), each time noted as remarkable for a firm the corpus flags as a deep-MiCA-readiness prospect.

**That absence was an instrumentation artefact.** The corpus was searching for *interviews with named marketing operators*. Bitpanda's public marketing signal was a **global brand campaign published on the firm's own blog**, in four languages, with named ambassadors, a named creative agency, named creator partners, and a self-labelled promotional disclaimer — sitting in the open the entire time.

This is **watch item (n) extending from classes 3 and 5 into class 4.** The class-4 fix applied on 07-25 widened the *time* range. This is a different axis: the *surface* range. A firm's own owned channels are a primary source and the class-4 sweep was not reading them.

> **Method consequence, and it should change the sweep before Phase 2 writes any absence sentence:** for every tracked firm, check the firm's **own blog / newsroom / press page** before recording "no public signal." An absence panel built only from interview and podcast surfaces measures media bookings, not marketing visibility.

## Three findings, one per theme

### THEME 3 — the agency panel is looking at the wrong agencies for this tier of firm

The report tracks an **18-agency crypto-native comparison panel** (Coinbound, MarketAcross, RZLT, NinjaPromo, Serotonin et al.) and builds `agency-overlap-matrix.csv` from it. That matrix currently resolves **8 tracked firms and exactly one overlap** (Sui — Coinbound + RZLT).

Bitpanda — a Tier-1 EU-licensed CASP with 7M+ users running TV, OOH and five football-club sponsorships — used **Serviceplan**, one of Europe's largest independent mainstream advertising groups, and **27km**, a German production house. Neither is a crypto-native agency. Neither is in the panel. Neither will ever appear in the matrix.

**The finding is not "the panel is wrong." It is that the panel measures a specific market segment, and the report must say which one.** The crypto-native agency panel captures the token/protocol/mid-tier-exchange market. At the top of Stratum 1, brand work moves to mainstream agencies and simply exits the corpus's field of view. Any Theme-3 claim of the form *"N tracked firms have no named agency relationship"* is therefore measuring **absence from the crypto-native panel**, not absence of an agency — and the report must state that distinction or the claim is false.

**Action for Phase 2:** add a column or a footnote distinguishing *no named agency* from *named non-crypto-native agency*. Bitpanda is the first documented instance and there are almost certainly more.

### THEME 4 — a live, dated, testable compliance artefact from a tracked EU firm

The page carries an appended disclaimer. Reproduced verbatim because it is exactly the object the AFM tests:

> "**Disclaimer** — Investing in digital assets carries risks, crypto is volatile. In extreme cases, **the invested amount may be lost completely**. **This Promotion does not constitute an investment advice** or an invitation to conclude a transaction. In no way is performance or results guaranteed. You should keep yourself informed and understand the risks involved in buying and holding digital assets."

> "Investing in crypto assets carries risks, and may not be suitable for all investors. Make sure to conduct your own research before making any investment."

**Read against the AFM's January-2025 defect classes** (`../regulator-filings/afm-casp-advertising-cost-information-baseline-2025-01.md`), this artefact scores well on the two the AFM was most specific about:

- **Risk warning specificity.** The AFM called *"investing/trading in crypto-assets has/involves risks"* **"too generic"**, and said one way to comply is to *"make clear what the relevant risk is, for example that you could lose your investment."* Bitpanda's disclaimer does precisely that: *"the invested amount may be lost completely."*
- **Advertisement identification** (Art. 66(2) MiCAR — advertisements "identified as such"). The page **self-labels: "This Promotion."**

**This is a good-practice exemplar, and the report should use it as one.** It is far more useful to a reader than another defect example: a named Tier-1 EU CASP, on its own channel, dated, showing what a compliant brand-campaign disclaimer looks like. Chapter 5's audit sweep has defect examples; it has been short of contrasting positives.

**Caveat stated, not smoothed.** This is the *blog post about* the campaign. **The corpus has not verified the disclaimer treatment on the campaign's actual TV, OOH, social and creator-delivered executions** — which is where the AFM's proportionality test (*"the warning is proportionate to the advertisement's context"*) and BaFin's finfluencer screening actually bite. **Do not print this as "Bitpanda's campaign is compliant."** The verified claim is narrower and still worth making: *"Bitpanda's own campaign announcement carries a specific-loss risk warning and self-identifies as a promotion."*

### THEME 4 / Theme 3 — two direct collisions with material already in this corpus

**(a) Arsenal FC ↔ the FCA Premier League sponsorship warning.**
`../regulator-filings/fca-premier-league-sponsorship-warning-2026-06.md` is already in the corpus. This campaign puts a MiCA-licensed CASP's brand on **Arsenal FC**, a Premier League club, while the firm states it is *"continuing to expand its UK presence."* A regulator warning about exactly that sponsorship channel, and a tracked firm active in it, now sit in the same corpus with dates nine months apart. Pair them in Chapter 4.

**(b) Creator partners ↔ BaFin's finfluencer screening.**
`../regulator-filings/bafin-risks-in-focus-crypto-finfluencer-2026-01.md` records BaFin committing to *"a random market screening of selected **German-speaking finfluencers** on the social media channels **YouTube and Instagram**"* and stating that *"Finfluencers – like any other person – are also subject to the market abuse rules of MiCAR."*

Bitpanda is Vienna-headquartered, its campaign is German-language-localised, its production house is German, and it names **Caro Daur** — a German creator whose primary surface is Instagram — as a campaign partner. **This is not an allegation of any breach and must not be written as one.** It is a documented instance of the precise configuration BaFin named four months later: German-speaking creators, Instagram, cryptoasset promotion, regulated CASP. The corpus previously held the regulator's stated posture and the operator practice as unconnected facts; they are now connected, dated, and both primary-sourced.
