## The agency-side statements (Theme 4 — recorded, and they are the analytically richest part)

**Philipp Thelen, Art Director, Holographik** — agency-side, *not* a class-4 operator statement, entered as agency-claim evidence:

> "A lot of crypto brands lean into hyper-volatility, which naturally reinforces ideas of speculation. For Sui, we wanted to position the brand as a trustworthy long-term infrastructure layer that could speak not only to crypto natives, but also institutions, developers and a much broader audience. **The visual language needed to say 'reliable stack' and 'data integrity' rather than 'hype.'**"

The article's own framing of the category problem:

> "Layer 1 brands have converged on a narrow set of cues – namely dark interfaces, neon gradients, abstract geometry and dense technical language, which is impressive at a glance but keeps audiences at arm's length. Holographik wanted to steer clear of what it calls the **'DeFi casino' aesthetic**."

**Ferdon and Thelen describe the same visual outcome and disagree completely about what it is.** For Ferdon, moving away from hype toward sterility is the gravitational pull she is fighting. For Thelen, moving away from hype toward *"reliable stack"* and *"data integrity"* is the brief, executed on purpose, to reach *"institutions, developers and a much broader audience."*

That is the most useful shape a theme can have: **two operators, ten weeks apart, converging on an identical aesthetic through opposite reasoning.** Chapter 5 should print both and let the tension stand. Note also the asymmetry that explains part of it — Coinbase sells a regulated consumer service; a Layer-1 foundation does not. The audience Thelen is de-risking *for* is institutional, not retail. Say so.

## Design-system specifics captured (evidence that this is a full system, not a logo tweak)

- **Symbol:** existing droplet retained and distilled — proportions adjusted, elongation reduced, geometry refined. Explicit brief to *"preserve the equity already sitting in the existing logo."* Thelen: *"The intention was to make the mark feel less decorative and more iconic."*
- **Colour:** reduced to essentials — **Blue/500** (electric) as hero against **Blue/900** (near-black), plus black, white, blue-toned greyscale. Thelen: *"The former feels energetic and future-facing without becoming overly synthetic or aggressive."*
- **Type:** **TWK Everett by Weltkern**, single typeface across the system — Helvetica lineage, legible in developer docs and dashboards, with *"sharp cuts and stroke endings"* for marketing and motion.
- **Icons:** derived from Everett's proportions/angles/stroke details — *"The icons don't sit on top of the brand language; they feel native to it."*
- **Gradients:** two (linear, radial), each three stacked layers — gradient + grain at Soft Light + blue overlay at Colour. Rules exist explicitly because *"teams across the ecosystem [are] producing content at volume"* and the studio needed *"to stop gradients drifting off-brand."*

**Theme-1 relevance of that last point:** a governance mechanism built for **distributed, high-volume content production across an ecosystem the foundation does not control**. That is a gate-stack artefact — brand control exercised through system design rather than approval workflow, because approval workflow is impossible at foundation scale.
