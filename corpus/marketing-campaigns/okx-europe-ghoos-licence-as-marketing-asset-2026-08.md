## Why this is the run's Theme-4 result

**The 08-08 run established that the post-deadline consequence of MiCA authorisation status was being administered on schedule by a private advertising platform and not at all by the authorities that own the perimeter** (`../ad-platform-gates/google-ads-mica-casp-gate-eu-eea-2026-08-08.md`). That was the null's first counterparty.

**This is its second, and it runs in the opposite direction.** Where Google converts the licence into an *access gate*, OKX converts it into a *promotional claim*. Forty days after the deadline, with no named marketing-side NCA action anywhere in the corpus, a Tier-1 CASP is publishing weekly, under a named executive's byline, that the reset **is working** — and using its own authorisation count as the differentiator.

**State the shape precisely, because it is the finding:**

> *In the post-deadline window, MiCA's observable marketing consequence is not enforcement against non-compliant promotion. It is the licence itself becoming promotional inventory for the compliant.*

Both counterparties — the ad platform and the licensee's own estate — are **private actors acting on the licence status**. Neither is a regulator. Neither tests whether any communication is fair, clear and not misleading.

**This also gives the report's Theme-3/Theme-4 competitive read a dated primary it did not have.** The corpus already holds `mica-competitive-capture-2026-06.md` and `mica-capture-campaign-lapse-checkpoint-2026-08-01.md`, which document MiCA-keyed campaigns *lapsing*. This is the counter-case: **a MiCA-keyed campaign that is still running, still being added to weekly, and escalating from acquisition language to institutional-trust language.**
