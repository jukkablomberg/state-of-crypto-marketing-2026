## What this is evidence of

### Theme 4 — a European tracked firm's sponsorship spend is pointed at the United States

Ledger is **Ledger SAS, 106 rue du Temple, Paris** — an EU-domiciled tracked firm. Its two verified sponsorship positions are an **NBA jersey patch** and a **US national TV buy on ESPN/ABC**, with secondary reach in Japan. Its CEO states on the same channel that **"The U.S. is Ledger's top market globally."**

**Stated as a dated sequence, with no causal claim:** across June 2025 → July 2026 — the twelve months running into the MiCA transitional deadline — the publicly visible sponsorship and broadcast spend of this EU-headquartered tracked firm lands in **non-EU jurisdictions**. The report can state that. It cannot state why, and must not imply regulatory avoidance: Ledger sells a hardware product, not a regulated crypto-asset service, and the corpus has no evidence connecting the two facts.

### Theme 4 — the June-2026 collision, recorded precisely and without allegation

The corpus holds `../regulator-filings/fca-premier-league-sponsorship-warning-2026-06.md` and `../operator-statements/sport-sponsorship-reset-2026-05.md`. In the **same month** as the FCA sponsorship warning (June 2026), Ledger's X-Games jersey placement went live in **Sacramento (June 26–28)** and its Spurs footer lockup was **re-uploaded (June 2026 asset path)**.

**This is a date coincidence between a UK regulator's warning about crypto sports sponsorship and a French firm's US/Japan sports sponsorship. It is not a compliance event and must never be printed as one.** It is recorded because Chapter 5 needs the timeline to be complete, and because it sharpens a real question: the sponsorship-scrutiny conversation in the corpus is entirely European, and the sponsorship *spending* being observed is not.

### Theme 3 — a whole portfolio with no agency visible, at a firm the matrix cannot see

**No agency is named anywhere in either announcement.** Media buying at this scale — national TV creative, venue build, athlete gifting logistics, a three-year league rights deal — is rarely fully in-housed, and Ledger separately names **"Ledger Studio"** as an internal capability.

Ledger appears in **zero** rows of `agency-overlap-matrix.csv`. Under watch **(q)** that is now a known-ambiguous reading: it may mean no agency, an in-house studio, or a mainstream agency the 18-agency crypto-native panel is structurally blind to. **Phase 2 must not read Ledger's empty matrix row as "agency-light."** Bitpanda/Serviceplan established the ambiguity; Ledger is the second instance and the first where the firm also self-declares an in-house studio.
