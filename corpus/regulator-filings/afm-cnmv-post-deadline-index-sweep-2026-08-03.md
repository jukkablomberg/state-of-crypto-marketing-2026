AFM has now been swept, at its own index, and **it replicated.** So did the second-likeliest breaker, CNMV — for the same reason and with the same result.

## 2. CNMV — the second-likeliest breaker, and why it does not break either

CNMV is the other NCA with a *published* advertising-conduct track record, and its instrument is even more marketing-specific than AFM's: **Circular 1/2022 of 10 January 2022, on advertising of crypto-assets presented as an investment** — a standalone crypto-advertising rule with a mass-campaign pre-notification regime.

**The precedent, captured this run (near-primary):** Cuatrecasas, *"La CNMV incoa expediente por publicidad de criptoactivos"*, **11 December 2023** — https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/cnmv-incoa-expediente-publicidad-criptoactivos

> **WINDOW FLAG — OUT OF SCOPE AS A CORPUS EVENT.** 11 December 2023 predates the methodology's December-2024 cut-off. It is entered here **as interpretive context for a live null**, not as an in-window item, and must not be cited in the report as a 2026 event.

What it establishes:

- CNMV opened its **first-ever sanction file (expediente sancionador)** under Circular 1/2022 against **MIOLO DESARROLLOS, S.L.**, a crypto-wallet provider, for two mass advertising campaigns run in September and November 2022.
- Alleged breaches: **Norma 5** (commercial communications must carry the explicit warning that crypto investment is unregulated, may be unsuitable for retail investors, and the entire sum may be lost) and **Norma 7** (mass campaigns — potential audience above 100,000 — must be notified to CNMV at least ten days before launch).
- Characterised as **four serious infractions** under Art. 292.4 in relation to Art. 240 bis of the Spanish Securities Market Law.
- CNMV **published the opening of the file deliberately**, to *"recordar la necesidad de seguir y respetar los preceptos"* of the Circular — i.e. it treats publicity of the case as part of the supervisory instrument.
- Scale of the surveillance behind it, as reported: **210+ supervisory actions, 1,300+ advertising pieces reviewed, ~200 information requirements** by November 2023.

**Why this matters to the null.** CNMV is a regulator that (i) wrote a bespoke crypto-advertising rule, (ii) built an advertising-review machine that reviews pieces in the thousands, (iii) has opened a marketing-conduct sanction file before, and (iv) publicises such files *on purpose*. **It is the profile of an NCA that would produce a named post-deadline marketing case if one existed.** No such 2026 case was reached this run.

> **NOT REACHED, NOT GUESSED:** CNMV's own `Advertencias` / sanctions register was not fetched this run (the 07 run's `cnmv-sanctions-register-read-2026-07.md` remains the corpus's CNMV primary). The CNMV `webservices/verdocumento` link for the 2023 comunicado was seen inside the Cuatrecasas text but not fetched. No URL was fabricated.

## 3. What this does to watch (v) and to the day-33 null

**Watch (v) — NCA warning-list / news-index sweep: 5 of 6 discharged.**

| NCA | swept | result |
|---|---|---|

| CONSOB (IT) | 08-02 | perimeter, not conduct |
| CySEC (CY) | 08-02 | perimeter, not conduct + conditional-mood statement |
| **AFM (NL)** | **08-03 (own index)** | **no crypto item in 109 days; 2 enforcement Measures, neither crypto** |
| CNMV (ES) | partial (07 register + 2023 precedent) | pre-window conduct precedent exists; no 2026 case reached |

**The day-33 null therefore survives its designated falsifier.** The scope is unchanged and must stay unchanged when printed: *no publicly named marketing-side enforcement action against an identified authorised CASP in the EU has been located in the 33 days since the transitional deadline.*

**And the null is now a stronger claim than it was, because of what did NOT happen.** The two NCAs with a demonstrated willingness to act on *advertising* rather than *authorisation* — AFM (thematic review, quantified defect rates, "leniency has ended") and CNMV (bespoke Circular, sanction-file precedent, deliberate publicity) — are the two that most plausibly would have broken it. Both were tested. Neither did.

**The honest counterweight, which must ship with the finding.** AFM's April release states that supervisory letters were going out and that ten international firms were being referred to home NCAs. **Supervisory letters and cross-border referrals are not public instruments.** So the correct reading of the null is *not* "regulators are doing nothing on crypto marketing". It is:

> **The post-deadline supervisory response to crypto marketing is, so far, running through non-public channels — supervisory letters, referrals, and perimeter warnings — and not through named, published conduct cases against authorised firms.**

That is a sharper claim than "silence", it is defensible from the primary sources above, and it is the version Phase 2 should print.
