## Why this enters class 3

BaFin does not name a firm and does not impose a sanction. What it does is **name the marketing channel itself as a supervisory priority for 2026, and commit to a screening programme against it.** That is a marketing-side supervisory posture from a Tier-1 NCA, stated in BaFin's own annual risk-priority document — the instrument that determines where its supervisory attention actually goes.

Read alongside `afm-casp-advertising-cost-information-review-2026-04.md` (AFM, 2026-04-16) and `esma-mica-transitional-period-end-2026-06.md`, it completes a picture the corpus previously only had in fragments: **the marketing surface is under structured supervisory attention across multiple NCAs, in the same window in which zero named marketing-side enforcement cases exist.**

## Verbatim capture — BaFin's stated line of approach

> "In order to protect investors, BaFin will continue to supervise market participants in its area of responsibility and inform consumers about the risks on the crypto market."

> "**It is not BaFin's responsibility to directly supervise finfluencers.** However, finfluencers are subject to supervisory requirements for certain activities, for example when they provide investment advice or make investment recommendations. Finfluencers – **like any other person** – are also subject to the market abuse rules of MiCAR, such as the prohibition of market manipulation and insider dealing."

> "Cryptoasset services may only be provided if BaFin has granted authorisation. BaFin will continue to supervise cryptoasset service providers (CASPs) with regard to their **compliance with rules of conduct and organisational requirements** and take measures where necessary."

**The committed action — a screening programme against the marketing channel:**

> "BaFin will expand the range of information it provides to consumers on trading in cryptoassets. **A random market screening of selected German-speaking finfluencers on the social media channels YouTube and Instagram will be the first step in this process.** BaFin will also continue to publish warnings about dubious crypto offers."

> "BaFin will survey consumers on their knowledge and experience with cryptoassets in 2026."

## Quantified evidence BaFin puts on the record

All figures from BaFin's own **representative consumer survey (2024)**, n=1,000 investors aged 18–45 who invested in the prior two years. Reproduced with BaFin's own sub-sample sizes because the report should cite the base, not just the headline.

| Comparison | Bought cryptoassets | Base |
|---|---|---|
| Regularly use social media for financial information | **43%** | n=375 |
| Do not use social media for financial information | **25%** | n=625 |
| Received information from a finfluencer | **~48%** ("almost half") | n=529 |
| Did not receive finfluencer information | **13%** | n=471 |
| Offered chat-group access by a finfluencer | **50%** | n=181 |
| Not offered chat-group access | **13%** | n=819 |

Also on the record:

> "…more than half of 18- to 45-year-old investors have received information from a finfluencer. About half of the investors who received finfluencer information bought the recommended product on this basis."

> "Dubious finfluencers can strongly influence the price by hyping or disparaging the investment and inducing consumers to act hastily, thereby **tapping into their 'fear of missing out'**."

> "In 2025, BaFin published **around 100 warnings** about dubious or fraudulent chat groups, most often on WhatsApp."

## Themes

- **Theme 4 (MiCA readiness — exposure surface).** Primary. BaFin explicitly extends MiCAR market-abuse rules to "any person," which is the mechanism by which a firm's KOL and affiliate programme becomes a regulated marketing surface with a supervisor already screening it. The 48%-vs-13% differential is the strongest public number available for *why* a supervisor treats influencer marketing as a first-order channel rather than a peripheral one.
- **Theme 3 (agency overlap matrix).** The tracked 18-agency panel sells KOL/influencer campaigns as a core product. This document is the supervisory counterweight to that product line, in Europe's largest market. Pair with the Kraken CGMO's dated repudiation of the KOL-tournament playbook (2026-05-19, `../operator-statements/kraken-gupta-growth-operating-model-2026-05.md`): one Tier-1 operator and one Tier-1 regulator arriving at the same channel from opposite directions, both in-window.
- **Theme 1 (function shape).** "Rules of conduct and organisational requirements" is the gate-stack, named by the supervisor that will inspect it.
