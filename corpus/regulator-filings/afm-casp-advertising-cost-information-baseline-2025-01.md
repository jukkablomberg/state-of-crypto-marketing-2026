## Why this is the highest-value class-3 entry available to the report

This is the **T0 leg of a two-point regulator time series on the same defect classes, at the same regulator, in the same market.**

- **T0 — 2025-01-21 (this file):** AFM publishes its findings and tells CASPs, in operationally specific terms, what compliant marketing and cost disclosure looks like. Nothing is measured or scored; the document is guidance plus a warning that the findings "could qualify as breaches" now that MiCAR is live.
- **T1 — 2026-04-16 (`afm-casp-advertising-cost-information-review-2026-04.md`):** the AFM returns, examines **33 CASPs**, and publishes **quantified defect rates** — 14 with significant advertising shortcomings, 19 with significant cost-information shortcomings — issues supervisory letters, refers ten international firms to their home-state NCAs, and puts an executive board member on the record: *"The period of leniency has ended."*

**Fifteen months separate the two. The named defect classes are the same in both.** That is the strongest longitudinal marketing-compliance evidence available to this report from any regulator, and it is now sourced at both ends.

The 2026 run of the study is itself framed by the AFM as a follow-up to this one. The report can therefore say something much harder than "regulators are paying attention": **a supervisor published specific, actionable guidance; waited fifteen months; re-tested the market; and found the same defects at scale.** That converts the report's "quiet copy" read from an inference about firm behaviour into a *measured non-response to published guidance*.

## Verbatim capture — the five advertising defect classes (AFM's own headings)

### 1. Warning of risks associated with transactions in crypto-assets

> "We saw various advertisements that do not warn of risks associated with transactions in crypto-asset."

And the standard the AFM sets for warnings that *are* present — this is the operationally load-bearing sentence:

> "The AFM considers the inclusion of a passage such as **'investing/trading in crypto-assets has/involves risks' to be too generic** to indicate the risks associated with transactions in crypto-assets. According to the AFM, one of the ways in which to comply with the standard would be to **make clear what the relevant risk is, for example that you could lose your investment**."

> "we consider it important that the warning is **proportionate to the advertisement's context**. Whether a warning is fair, clear and not misleading depends on the context, both in terms of content and presentation, and may differ per advertisement."

### 2. Presentation of historical returns

> "In some cases, the advertisements did not use a representative period because of the shortness of the period. If advertisements outline historical or actual returns, we expect them to be based on a period that is representative."

### 3. Presentation of staking rewards

> "The AFM considers it important that such advertisements mentioning the (possible) advantages of crypto-assets should also mention the associated risks and possible limitations. An important limitation in this context, for example, is that the **suggested rewards from staking services may be variable**, such as is the case if a CASP can adjust these rewards at any given time. We also deem it important for CASPs to mention **whether eligibility for such rewards is subject to certain conditions**."

### 4. Presentation of promotions

> "We encountered advertisements offering to waive transaction fees for clients who trade above a certain volume. These promotions usually featured a limited period. We deem it important that promotional ads **show the timespan in a clear and fair manner**."

> "some advertisements refer to '**transaction credit**' … In using this term, we consider it important that the impression is not unfairly created that these are credits that can be used to purchase crypto-assets."
> *(AFM footnote: the Dutch term is 'transactietegoed'.)*

### 5. "Saving" or trading "safely" in cryptos

> "Some of the advertisements examined by the AFM talk about trading '**safely**' in cryptos, or '**savings**' in crypto-assets. The AFM stresses that fair, clear and non-misleading information disclosure means that **risks should not be trivialised**, especially given the volatility of crypto-assets. If advertisements talk about trading 'safely' in crypto-assets, it is essential to provide further explanation or context to the meaning thereof."

> "In addition, clients may be misled by the term 'savings'. In the Netherlands, the term 'savings' has a **strong connotation with holding funds in a bank, by virtue of the deposit-guarantee scheme**."
> *(AFM footnote: the Dutch term is 'sparen'.)*

**Direct continuity with T1.** The 2026-04-16 review singles out *"statements referring to 'safe' trading in crypto, without further explanation or clarification of the associated risks"* — the **same defect, in near-identical wording, fifteen months later**, now with a count attached to it.

## Verbatim capture — the three cost-information defect classes

### 6. No policies on pricing, costs and fees on the public website

> "We came across a few cases that did not show any policies on pricing, costs and/or fees on the public website. … we deem it important that CASPs not only mention the general fact that pricing, costs and/or fees may apply. They should also give clients **actual insight into the specific pricing, costs and/or fees** charged by a CASP. In general, we consider the use of a **cost schedule and/or cost calculator** to be good practice."

### 7. No prominent place given to policies on pricing, costs and fees

> "There were several cases in which the cost information was **spread across the website as opposed to being posted on one single page**. For example, on the landing page, on specific pages about costs, under so-called knowledge academies, under 'frequently asked questions' (FAQs) or support centres and/or in the General Terms and Conditions (GT&C). We note that we generally **do not consider knowledge academies, FAQs, support centres and/or the GT&C to be a prominent place**."

**The testable standard — quote this in the report; it is the most operationally usable sentence any NCA has published on this surface:**

> "One way of ensuring that the information is in a prominent place is to locate it **one click away from the homepage, or two clicks if using a drop-down menu**."

### 8. No plausible components of cost in the policies

> "By plausible cost components, we mean in any case usual costs such as **deposit and/or withdrawal fees, transaction fees and current costs**. … **Failing to mention such costs, while clients do pay them, could suggest CASPs to be misleading their clients**."

## Legal hooks the AFM cites (carry these into the citation appendix)

| Provision | What it requires (AFM's framing) |
|---|---|
| **Art. 66(2) MiCAR** | Information to clients — including in advertisements — must be **fair, clear and not misleading**; advertisements must be identified as such; CASPs must not, deliberately or negligently, mislead clients as to the real or perceived advantages of crypto-assets. |
| **Art. 66(3) MiCAR** | CASPs must **warn clients of the risks** associated with transactions in crypto-assets. |
| **Art. 66(4) MiCAR** | CASPs must make policies on **pricing, costs and fees publicly available, in a prominent place** on their website. |
| **Arts. 7, 29, 53 MiCAR** | Advertisement requirements tied to a public offer / admission to trading. **Explicitly out of scope of this AFM study**, which addresses only the generic fair-clear-non-misleading standard (AFM footnote 1). |
| Comparator | AFM cross-references **Art. 24(3) MiFID II** and **s. 4:19(2) Wft** as "separate standards" bearing similarities it will consider "where relevant and appropriate." |

## Scope note — what this document is and is not

**It is not an enforcement case.** No firm is named. No sanction is imposed. It is guidance derived from pre-MiCAR exploratory fieldwork, published in the month after MiCAR entered full application.

**It does not weaken the report's headline null.** The corpus's day-27 count of **zero named marketing-side enforcement cases post-deadline** is untouched by this entry.

**What it does establish** is that the guidance existed, in public, in specific terms, from January 2025 — which is what makes the April 2026 defect rates readable as a *response* to guidance rather than as a first encounter with it.

## AFM's stated forward posture (January 2025)

> "MiCAR fully entered into force on 30 December 2024, and we expect CASPs to ensure that their information disclosure, including advertisements, meets the applicable standards. We note that the previously mentioned findings under MiCAR **could qualify as breaches**, which is why we urge CASPs to apply the guidance provided. **We will be supervising advertisements** and in doing so, we also consider it important how parties define their policies on this."

> "The AFM exerts **risk-driven supervision, both in terms of our regular supervision as in thematic studies.** … We will keep you up to date on current developments and **will conduct further studies**."

The AFM announced the thematic-study instrument in January 2025 and executed it in April 2026. Both legs are now in the corpus.
