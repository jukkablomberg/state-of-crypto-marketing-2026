## Why this matters more than its size

This is **the first instrument in the corpus in which a European regulator writes, in its own words, about promotional conduct itself** — labelling of paid partnerships, the legal weight of disclaimers, the boundary between a post and regulated advice, and the specific risk framing owed to crypto.

Every prior class-3 admission concerned **authorisation status** (registers, warning lists, transitional-period statements) or **product intervention**. Those tell the report who is licensed. **This one tells the report what the regulator thinks a promotion may and may not say.** That is the report's actual subject.

**It is also, and this is the finding, not addressed to licensees.**

The factsheet's audience is finfluencers — second-person throughout: *"you're still responsible for what you post"*, *"if you're not licensed or authorised to provide investment advice, don't give personalised recommendations"*. **Not one sentence is addressed to a CASP, an issuer, or any authorised firm.** And it disclaims its own legal force:

## Verbatim — ESMA's own words, EN factsheet, complete on the crypto-relevant points

> **"Promoting a financial product or service isn't like promoting shoes or watches. It can have significant negative financial consequences for your followers. You need to take extra precautions."**

**§2 — paid partnerships (Theme 3, KOL economics):**

> *"If you're getting money, gifts or perks to promote something, don't hide it – say it loud and clear. **Not in tiny text. Not just hashtags.** Use words such as **'Ad', 'Paid partnership' or 'Sponsored'**, or use the platform's integrated 'ad' banner.
> Also, if you already invest in what you're posting about, or you could benefit if others buy it – say that too."*

**§3 — crypto named explicitly, first of two times:**

> *"Some investments commonly marketed by finfluencers – such as contracts for difference, forex, futures, certain crowdfunding initiatives, or **volatile cryptocurrencies** – can carry very high risk, including **the possibility of losing 100 % of the capital you invest.**
> Make sure what you say is **true, fair, clear and not misleading**. Distinguish between facts and opinions."*

**§4 — crypto named a second time; the urgency prohibition:**

> *"Understand the features of what you're promoting, especially if you're giving visibility to risky products such as contracts for difference, forex, futures or **cryptos**. Be honest. **Highlight the risks, not just the rewards. Do not add pressure or create urgency by saying things such as 'get rich fast'.** Because people can also lose money – fast.
> If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is. … **always check whether the firm or platform is authorised before you post about it. If not, you could be an accomplice to a scam.**"*

**§5 — the disclaimer point, which is the one most directly load-bearing for the report:**

> *"Telling people what to invest in – or what to avoid – can count as investment advice. If you want to do this, you'll need a license granted by your national competent authority. …
> Even sharing your opinion publicly about whether a share or **crypto** will go up or down – or promoting an investment strategy – can count as an investment recommendation, to which rules can apply. **In certain contexts, providing training or educational content might be regarded as advice or recommendations.**
> **Disclaimers such as 'This is not investment advice' will not protect you in these cases.**"*

**§6 and the summary box:**

> *"If you don't fully understand a product – don't talk about it as if you do."*
> *"How to play it safe: Be honest. Be clear. Don't fake expertise. Don't mislead and don't advise. Say if you're being paid. **Think before you post: if in doubt, don't post.**"*

## What CONSOB adds that ESMA's own document does not

CONSOB's press release is the **national amplification** the 08-11 CANDIDATE was built around, and it is now first-party. Its own characterisation:

> *"Promoting a financial product or service on social media is not the same as promoting shoes or watches, explains the factsheet **published on the ESMA and Consob websites**."*

> *"**Disclaimers such as 'This is not investment advice' are not enough to protect you from the legal consequences of unauthorised promotions.**"*

> *"One section is dedicated to high-risk products, such as contracts for difference (CFDs), currency trading, futures, certain crowdfunding initiatives and **volatile crypto-assets**."*

**Note the co-publication claim:** CONSOB states the factsheet is published on **both** the ESMA and CONSOB websites. That makes CONSOB a **publisher**, not merely a commentator — which is the strongest form the "amplification" hypothesis could take, and it comes from CONSOB's own pen.
