## Why this is the corpus's most operationally specific ESMA marketing document

Every other ESMA object in this corpus is either a *perimeter* instrument (wind-down, transitional period, product intervention scope) or a *warning*. **This one tells CASPs, in a Dos/Don'ts table, what they may and may not put in a marketing communication.** It is the closest thing the EU has published to a marketing-conduct rulebook for CASPs short of MiCAR Art. 66 itself — and it is a year old.

**And it names, as a DON'T, precisely the marketing move this corpus has documented tracked Tier-1 firms making in 2026.**

## Verbatim extraction — the marketing-relevant clauses

**The 'halo effect', defined by the regulator:**

> "A CASP regulated status under MiCA may create a 'halo effect' that may often serve to provide potentially misguided reassurance in relation to the unregulated products and/or services offered by that CASP or related-entities offering unregulated product and/or services through the same platform."

**The sentence that does the work — regulated status as a marketing argument, named as a risk by ESMA:**

> "**Some CASPs may even use their regulated status under MiCA as a marketing argument** and encourage the confusion between regulated and unregulated products and services."

**The standing obligation, tied to Art. 66(2):**

> "they must comply with their obligation to ensure that all information, **including marketing communications**, addressed to clients or potential clients is fair, clear and not misleading."

### The Dos / Don'ts table — transcribed as published

| DOS | DON'TS |
|---|---|
| "The regulatory status of the product and/or service is clearly and effectively communicated in all dealings with clients, and at every stage of the sales process. For example, **all marketing communications should indicate clearly if a product and/or service offered by a CASP is regulated or not.** Such indication should be clearly visible to clients and prospective clients." | "The terminology used implies that a product or service falling outside of the scope of MiCA or other EU sectoral legislation is regulated in any way where this is not the case. Information as to the unregulated status of a product or service is merely visible in the Terms and Conditions only." |
| "Any information on a CASP's website related to unregulated activities is clearly distinguished from regulated activities. CASPs have separate sections on any website they operate for regulated activities and any other activities which they carry out. Client documentation is distinguished accordingly…" | "**The CASP's regulatory status is used as a promotional tool.** When engaging in unregulated activities, information provided to the client or potential client, **including marketing materials** and other documentation, includes a reference to the CASP being authorised/regulated by an NCA." |
| "The entity offering each service and/or product is clearly communicated, their legal and regulatory status established and conflicts of interest are managed in line with MiCA requirements, as applicable." | "It is unclear which entity is providing the unregulated service and/or product at the time of entering into the related agreement and the related risks are obfuscated. The differentiation between service providers is made exclusively in the Terms and Conditions." |
| "Before the client is provided unregulated services and/or products, there is a pop-up window stating that these are unregulated services/products. This pop-up includes a check the box approach requiring that the client has read the information provided… before the client is actually able to receive the unregulated services and/or products." | "Unregulated entities (be they part of the same group as the CASP or not) offer services and/or products regulated or unauthorised under MiCA through the CASP's interface with clients." |

**Legal anchors cited by ESMA:** Art. 66(1) MiCA (act fairly, professionally, in clients' best interests); **Art. 66(2) MiCA** (all information, including marketing communications, fair, clear, not misleading); Regulation (EU) 2023/1114; Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II) as the sectoral comparator.

## The cross-read — this is why the file matters to the report

The corpus already holds **`../marketing-campaigns/okx-europe-ghoos-licence-as-marketing-asset-2026-08.md`** (captured 2026-08-10): two dated, bylined posts by **Erald Ghoos, CEO of OKX Europe**, on OKX's own `/learn/` estate, in which the firm's **three authorisations are the differentiator** —

> "OKX Europe holds three separate authorisations: a MiCA CASP licence, MiFID II authorisation, and a Payment Institution licence… answerable to European regulators across all 30 EEA states."

It also holds **`../marketing-campaigns/mica-competitive-capture-2026-06.md`** (OKX 8% / Coinbase 5% / Kraken €1M EEA capture campaigns keyed to licensed status) and **`../marketing-campaigns/kraken-institutional-mica-counterparty-2026-06.md`**.

**The corpus has been describing a 2026 pattern — the licence becoming promotional inventory — without knowing that ESMA had already, in July 2025, named that exact pattern and placed it in the DON'T column.**

**State the shape precisely, and state its limits precisely:**

> *ESMA's guidance does not prohibit a CASP from stating that it is authorised. The DON'T is narrower and sharper: **using the regulatory status as a promotional tool in connection with unregulated activities**, and failing to distinguish, at every stage of the sales process, which products the authorisation actually covers.*

**This changes what Theme 4 can say.** The report's post-deadline finding to date has been *"no EU NCA has adjudicated a marketing communication."* That remains true. But the accompanying implication — that the EU has said nothing operational about crypto marketing conduct — **is now false, and this corpus would have printed it.** ESMA published a marketing Dos/Don'ts table thirteen months ago. **The gap is not guidance. The gap is enforcement.** That is a materially better and more defensible framing than the one the corpus was carrying into Phase 2.

## What is NOT claimed

1. **No compliance verdict on any firm.** This file does **not** assess whether OKX's, Kraken's, or Coinbase's licence-keyed communications breach this guidance. Several of those communications concern **regulated** services, where the DON'T (which is scoped to unregulated activities) may not bite at all. **The corpus records; it does not adjudicate.** Phase 2 must not let this cross-read drift into an accusation.
2. **A Statement is not a rule and not an enforcement action.** It is supervisory guidance addressed to CASPs. It creates no new obligation beyond Art. 66. It is **not** evidence of any NCA action, and it does **not** break watch (b).
3. **No claim that this is the only such ESMA document.** It is the only one this corpus has found, and the index it was found in is defective (see `_esma-news-index-pagination-instrument-2026-08-15.md`). **There may be more. The sweep is not complete and this file does not pretend it is.**
4. **The related-documents table on the landing page rendered empty** in the fetch. Any additional annexes are **not reached, not guessed.**
