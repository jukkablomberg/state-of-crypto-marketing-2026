## Why this is the most consequential class-3 entry since the transitional-period chain

This is the **first marketing-side supervisory instrument in the corpus in which a national competent authority audited crypto firms' actual advertising and published quantified findings.** It is *not* a named enforcement case — no firm is named, no sanction is imposed — so it does **not** break the report's day-N "no named marketing-side enforcement case" count. It is a distinct and previously unrecorded instrument: the **thematic supervisory review**, sitting between register-building and casework.

It also arrives from the one major EU jurisdiction that is **roughly a year further into the post-transition regime than France, Italy or Austria.** The AFM's own text dates the end of the Dutch transitional period to **30 June 2025** — twelve months before the 1 July 2026 deadline the rest of this corpus is organised around. The Netherlands is therefore usable as a **leading-indicator jurisdiction**: what the AFM is doing now is a reasonable forecast of what the AMF, BaFin, CONSOB and CySEC do in H1-2027.

## Verbatim capture

On the supervisory posture — **Hanzo van Beusekom, executive board member, AFM**:

> "We see that some companies are really doing their best, but at the same time too many CASPs are lagging behind. Now is the time for the sector to take responsibility. **The period of leniency has ended.**"

On the review's scope and results (AFM, own words):

> "For the study, the advertisements and cost information of **33 CASPs** were examined. Among the companies examined, significant shortcomings in advertising were found at **14 CASPs**, and significant shortcomings in cost information at **19 CASPs**. The Dutch firms concerned will soon receive a supervisory letter. For the **ten international firms**, we will inform the relevant national regulators of the shortcomings identified."

On the specific defect pattern:

> "Many advertisements were insufficiently balanced about the risks. For example, we still saw statements referring to **'safe' trading in crypto**, without further explanation or clarification of the associated risks."

> "We have also come across some cases where information about costs is not publicly available in a prominent place on the website. Sometimes the information could not be found at all on the website, or could only be found by using external search engines."

On enforcement intent:

> "…anyone who continues to violate the standards for the provision of information should expect enforcement action."

> "We will continuously monitor this and take enforcement actions where necessary."

**AFM's five stated points for improvement (verbatim list):**

1. Avoid misleading claims and ensure that information is clearly substantiated
2. Disclose the risks in a balanced way
3. Ensure that information on costs is easy to find
4. Provide consumers with clear and transparent information on costs
5. Ensure clear information disclosure on services within and outside the scope of supervision

## The load-bearing sentence — a regulator documents the marketing chill

> "In the current study, there were several CASPs that were **hesitant to engage in marketing activities or did not have any marketing communications in the period under review**, due to the necessary mitigation measures after the end of the transitional period on 30 June 2025. When these CASPs resume their marketing activities, it is essential that they ensure that the information they provide, including in advertising, complies with MiCAR standards."

**Why this matters more than the shortfall counts.** This corpus has been *inferring* a post-deadline marketing chill from artefacts — quiet copy, vacant CMO seats, an operator-statement drought, absence-panel silence. Here a supervisor states, in a primary document, that it **directly observed** firms going quiet on marketing as a mitigation response to the end of a MiCA transitional period. The absence finding is no longer only the report's inference from what firms did not publish; it is corroborated by an independent regulator that had access to the same market and wrote it down.

Cross-reference: `../../findings/00-opening-register-first-cases-later.md` ("the empty chairs", "the quiet copy") and the operator-statement drought recorded through May–July 2026.

## Themes

- **Theme 4 (MiCA readiness — exposure surface).** Primary. Quantified: 14/33 firms with significant advertising shortcomings, 19/33 with cost-disclosure shortcomings. The "safe trading" superlative the AFM singles out is the same defect class the report's own Chapter-5 audit sweep found live on public pages — independent corroboration of the audit's central claim from a supervisor's own testing.
- **Theme 1 (function shape).** The chill observation is direct evidence that the marketing function's *output* is being gated by compliance mitigation, not merely reviewed by it.
- **Theme 5 (by sequence).** Firms that went quiet on marketing during the mitigation window are the same population in which marketing-team cuts are not being named — worth pairing in Phase 2, carefully and without asserting causation.

## Scope and dedup notes

- **Not an enforcement action.** No firm is named; no sanction is imposed. Supervisory letters to Dutch firms and referrals to home-state NCAs for ten international firms are **pre-enforcement** instruments. The report's "zero named marketing-side enforcement cases" count is **unaffected** and must not be quietly softened by this entry.
- **Net-new.** No AFM file existed in `corpus/regulator-filings/` before this run (contents were ESMA, AMF, FCA, Binance-EU-exit). Repo-wide greps for `afm`, `beusekom`, `zikmund` returned only run-record and methodology mentions of the AFM as a named NCA, never this document.
- **Jurisdictional date care.** The AFM states the Dutch transitional period ended **30 June 2025**. Do not conflate this with the 1 July 2026 EU-wide end-of-transition chain already captured (ESMA 23-06-2026, AMF 23-06-2026). Member-state transition lengths differ; this file is the earlier Dutch clock.
- **Follow-up study.** The AFM describes this as a follow-up to an early-2025 study conducted when MiCAR had just taken effect. **That earlier study is not yet in the corpus and should be located** — a two-point AFM time series on the same defect classes would be the strongest longitudinal marketing-compliance evidence available to this report from any regulator.
