## Why this is the most consequential class-3 item the corpus has captured since the deadline

**For 36 days this corpus has recorded a named marketing-side enforcement absence across six NCAs plus ESMA's own index.** Every prior run could offer only structural explanations for it — the perimeter-shaped toolkit (CONSOB, 08-05), the redirected supervisory priority (ESMA's DORA/custody CSA, 07-08), the non-public-channel hypothesis (08-03).

**Today a supervisor gave a reason, on the record, in his own name.** It is none of the above, and it is better evidence than any of them:

> The AMF "has also avoided setting an aggressive deadline for unlicensed exchanges to halt operations in an effort to limit such scams."
> — The Block, reporting the FT, 2026-08-06

**The absence is, at least in France, a deliberate supervisory choice with a stated consumer-protection rationale.** Enforcement forbearance is being exercised *because* abrupt shutdown of unlicensed venues pushes users toward fraudulent "alternatives." That is a *reason for the null*, not merely a *description* of it — and it is the first one in the corpus attributable to a named official.

**Phase-2 consequence, stated plainly:** the report may no longer characterise the post-deadline period as regulators being slow, quiet, or absent. The corpus now holds three compatible, separately-sourced components:

1. **Structural** — the legislated marketing instruments are perimeter-scoped (CONSOB art. 36 c.2-*quaterdecies*, 08-05).
2. **Prioritisation** — the first coordinated post-deadline supervisory action targets custody and operational resilience, not marketing (ESMA CSA, 07-08).
3. **Deliberate forbearance, newly evidenced today** — at least one NCA is consciously declining to force an abrupt halt, and says why.

**Never print "silence."**

## The marketing-side reading (this is why it is class 3 and not general news)

**1. The first EU-level regulator-brand abuse recorded in this corpus is a *marketing* abuse.** Scammers are using ESMA's logo *to promote* fraudulent offerings, and impersonating AMF staff to redirect assets. The regulator's own identity has become a creative asset in someone else's acquisition funnel. Where the corpus has so far asked "does the regulator police marketing?", this item shows the regulator's mark itself being deployed as a trust signal in unlicensed promotion.

**2. The licence has become the marketing claim — and therefore the attack surface.** The AFM's warning is precisely that consumers *searching for a licensed alternative* are the target population. MiCA authorisation is now the thing consumers shop for; "we are licensed" is now the highest-converting claim in EU crypto marketing; and that is exactly why it is being counterfeited. This converges with, and independently corroborates, the already-captured Google Ads France change of 2026-07-01 (AMF DASP registration no longer accepted; MiCA CASP authorisation required) — **the licence is becoming the marketing credential, enforced by platforms and counterfeited by fraudsters, ahead of any regulator case about marketing conduct.**

**3. Theme-4 register arithmetic, dated today.** Per The Block, "roughly 320 entities have obtained MiCA licenses, according to a list updated on Aug. 5" by ESMA, against an FT/VASPnet estimate that **more than 1,700 would have to cease operations**. **CROSS-CHECK, LOGGED NOT RESOLVED:** NorthPoint's own primary-register read on 2026-08-06 returned **324 authorised** (329 rows → 324 distinct pairs, latest `ac_lastupdate` 2026-08-04), *including two firms whose authorisation has already ended*. "Roughly 320" and 324 are compatible; they are not the same number, and neither is safe to print without stating which register snapshot and which de-duplication rule produced it. **`[VERIFY]` before any Phase-2 use.**

**4. Binance, already in the corpus, is named as the notable failure case** — consistent with `binance-mica-eu-exit-2026-06.md`. No new Binance fact; no new row.
