## What it says (as summarised on the AMF page, verbatim where quoted)

- ESMA "has issued a statement reminding firms of their obligation to **assess whether newly offered products fall within the scope of existing product intervention measures on binary options**."
- It "responds to the **growing popularity of prediction markets - or event contracts - and increasing retail participation globally**."
- Event contracts are "products whose financial outcome is binary (a fixed payout or no payout at all) and depends on a yes-or-no answer to a question about a future event… **Event contracts may (also) qualify as bets under national gambling legislation.**"
- **The marketing-side operative sentence:** "Where event contracts are financial instruments, they classify as derivatives and, given the binary outcome, fall within the scope of the existing national product intervention measures on binary options adopted by national competent authorities **prohibiting their marketing, distribution or sale to retail clients**."
- "The distribution of event contracts qualifying as financial instruments in the EU **requires an authorisation as investment firm, even where only distributed to non-retail clients**."

## Why this is a class-3 corpus item and not noise

This is the **first EU-level regulator instrument captured in the post-deadline window that operates directly on a marketing prohibition**. The binary-option product-intervention measures are, on their face, a ban on *marketing, distribution or sale* to retail. ESMA is extending that perimeter, by interpretation, to a fast-growing product category.

**Scoping discipline — do not overclaim.** This does **not** break the corpus's standing day-N finding. That finding is scoped to *named marketing-side enforcement actions against identified firms*. This is a supervisory reminder addressed to firms generally, names no firm, imposes no sanction, and is not a MiCA instrument (it is MiFID II product-intervention). The correct reading:

> **Post-deadline, EU regulators have been active on the marketing perimeter without taking a single named marketing-side enforcement action.** Activity ≠ enforcement. The report must hold both halves.
