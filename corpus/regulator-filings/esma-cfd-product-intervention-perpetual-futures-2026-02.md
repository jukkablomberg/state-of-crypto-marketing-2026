## What the statement does

ESMA reminds firms that derivatives **"often marketed as perpetual futures or perpetual contracts"** that give leveraged exposure to underlying values — **crypto-assets such as Bitcoin named explicitly** — are **likely to fall within the scope of existing national CFD product-intervention measures.**

**Verbatim, from ESMA's own page:**

> "The statement responds to the increased offering of derivatives, **often marketed as perpetual futures or perpetual contracts**, that provide leveraged exposure to underlying values, **including crypto-assets such as Bitcoin**. These financial instruments are likely to fall within the scope of the existing national product intervention measures on CFDs adopted by national competent authorities."

> "Where these derivatives meet the definition of a CFD, they are subject to the applicable product intervention requirements, including **leverage limits, a mandatory risk warning, a margin close-out and negative balance protection, and the prohibition of monetary and non-monetary benefits.**"

> "given their complexity, **derivatives require a narrow target market, supported by an aligned distribution strategy**"

> "when providing non-advised services, an appropriateness assessment must be carried out in accordance with the relevant requirements for complex financial instruments"

> "firms should take appropriate steps to identify, prevent, or manage **conflicts of interest** that may arise from the offering of these products."

## Why a product-intervention statement is a marketing document

Three of the enumerated requirements are marketing-surface obligations, not back-office ones:

1. **Mandatory risk warning** — a prescribed element of the promotional communication itself.
2. **Prohibition of monetary and non-monetary benefits** — a direct ban on the **acquisition-incentive** mechanic. This is the same mechanic the corpus captured running at scale in the EEA six weeks before the MiCA deadline: **OKX's 8% transfer bonus, Coinbase's 5% transfer bonus, Kraken's €1M prize draw** (`../marketing-campaigns/mica-competitive-capture-2026-06.md`). **Where a product is in CFD scope, that entire promotional category is prohibited.**
3. **"A narrow target market, supported by an aligned distribution strategy"** — a targeting-and-channel constraint written into the product governance rules. This is the regulator specifying who the marketing may reach.

**Read alongside `../regulator-filings/mas-ps-g02-dpt-public-promotion-guidelines.md`, a jurisdictional pattern now has three points rather than two:**

| Regulator | What it regulates | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **MAS** | marketing **reach** — the general-public channel is closed *ex ante* | PS-G02 §2.1–2.3 |
| **ESMA (MiCA Art. 66)** | marketing **content** — fair, clear, not misleading; adjudicated after the fact | Art. 66(2); the 11-Jul-2025 halo statement |
| **ESMA (CFD PI measures)** | marketing **mechanics** — specific promotional instruments (inducements) prohibited outright, warnings mandated, target market narrowed | this statement |

**The third row is the one the report has been missing.** The corpus's post-deadline narrative — *"the EU adjudicates content and has not yet adjudicated"* — was drawn from a two-row table. **The EU does not only adjudicate content; in the derivatives perimeter it also bans specific promotional mechanics outright, and it said so five months before the deadline.**
