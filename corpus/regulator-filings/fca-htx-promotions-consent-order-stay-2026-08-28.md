## 1. ⭐⭐ 🔴 THE FINDING — A FINANCIAL REGULATOR HAS NAMED THE MARKETING FUNCTION ITSELF AS A DEFENDANT CLASS

The claim, issued in the Chancery Division on **21 October 2025**, names five defendants. Four of the five are PERSONS UNKNOWN, defined by what they *do* rather than who they are. **The fourth is defined purely as a marketing function**, verbatim from the sealed order:

> **"(4) PERSONS UNKNOWN (who are the persons currently in control of promotions on behalf of the HTX Exchange on any of the following social media platforms and/or messenger services: X, Facebook, Instagram, Telegram, TikTok, YouTube, Discord, Medium and/or LinkedIn)"**

And the fifth extends that class **forward in time**, verbatim:

> **"(5) PERSONS UNKNOWN (who are such additional persons who on or before 31 October 2028 … become controllers on behalf of the HTX Exchange of accounts on any of the following social media platforms and/or messenger services (X, Facebook, Instagram, Telegram, Tik Tok, You Tube, Discord, Medium and/or LinkedIn)"**

**This is the sharpest single artifact this corpus holds, and it belongs to Theme 1 as much as Theme 4.**

Every other regulator document in this corpus addresses the *firm*: the firm must not market, the firm's promotion must carry a risk warning, the firm's authorisation permits these services in these states. This one addresses **whoever is holding the social accounts** — nine named platforms, enumerated — as a party to litigation, and pre-emptively binds their successors for three years.

> 🟢 **PERMITTED, and it is close to verbatim:** *In the United Kingdom's first enforcement action against a crypto exchange over marketing, the fourth defendant is not a company or a named individual. It is "the persons currently in control of promotions" on nine named platforms — and the fifth defendant is whoever holds those accounts at any point before 31 October 2028.*
> 🔴 **PROHIBITED:** any suggestion that a named individual at HTX or anywhere else is a defendant. **The defendant classes are unidentified by construction** — the FCA's own press release says HTX *"operates an opaque organisational structure, hiding the identities of its owners and the operators of its website."* No person may be named against this document.

## 2. 🔴 THE STAY — AND ITS DATE IS A PROBLEM AND AN OPPORTUNITY FOR THE REPORT

The 24 August 2026 Consent Order, in full on the operative points:

> **"1. The Proceedings are stayed as between the Claimant and the First Defendant from the date of this Order until 8 September 2026 for the parties to try to settle the dispute by alternative dispute resolution or other means (the 'Stay')."**
> **"2. If a settlement has been reached, the Claimant and First Defendant shall lodge a draft Consent Order indicating the same and request that the Court make that Order."**
> **"3. Upon termination or expiration of the Stay, if no settlement has been reached the default provisions of the Civil Procedure Rules will apply thereafter."**
> **"4. There shall be no order as to costs."**

**The stay expires 8 September 2026 — seven days after this report ships on 1 September.**

> 🟢 **PERMITTED, and the report should state it as a dated forward marker any reader can check:** *As this report goes to press, the UK's first crypto-marketing enforcement action is stayed by consent until 8 September 2026 while the FCA and Huobi Global S.A. attempt settlement. If no settlement is reached, the Civil Procedure Rules resume.*
> 🔴 **PROHIBITED:** predicting the outcome, characterising the likelihood of settlement, or describing the case as "settled", "dropped" or "won". **Nothing in the record supports any of those.**

**⚠ The stay binds two parties, not five.** Paragraph 1 is scoped *"as between the Claimant and the First Defendant"* — Huobi Global S.A. only. **Defendants 2–5, including the promotions-controllers class, are not party to the stay.** Do not write "the case is paused" without that qualification.

### 🔴 The trade press's characterisation was wrong, and reading the primary is what corrected it

The search results that surfaced this item said the High Court had paused proceedings **"until late August"** to let the parties settle. That describes the *previous* order (Chief Master Shuman, 25 June 2026) and was already superseded when it was published. **The current order runs from 24 August to 8 September.** A report that had cited the secondary source would have shipped on 1 September saying a stay had already expired that in fact had eleven days left to run.

**Watch (vv), eighth consecutive vindication, and the first on a class-3 item:** a number — here a date — is not safe until someone has read its citation.

## 3. What the FCA alleges, verbatim from its own press release (P1)

The corpus records the allegations **as allegations**, attributed, and asserts none of them.

- The proceedings are for *"illegally promoting cryptoasset services to UK consumers."*
- *"Advertising cryptoassets on social media or websites without complying with these rules is a criminal offence."*
- *"The FCA previously warned about HTX's illegal promotion of crypto services to UK consumers. However, it has continued to publish financial promotions in breach of these rules on its website and on social media platforms, including TikTok, X, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube."*
- *"Repeated attempts by the FCA to engage with HTX have been ignored."*
- *"Since issue of the proceedings, HTX has taken steps to restrict new UK customers from registering an account. However, existing UK users can still log in and access unlawful financial promotions, and HTX has given no assurance that the changes will be permanent."*
- The FCA *"requested social media companies block HTX's social media accounts to UK-based consumers and requested the removal of HTX applications from the Google Play and Apple stores in the UK."*

**Named regulator statement — Steve Smart, joint executive director of enforcement and market oversight, FCA** (this is a *regulator* statement and is filed under class 3; it does **not** qualify for class 4, which requires a marketing operator at a tracked firm):

> *"HTX's conduct stands in stark contrast to the majority of firms working to comply with the FCA's regime. **This is the first time we've taken enforcement action against a crypto firm illegally marketing their products to UK consumers.** We'll continue to act against firms who ignore our rules."*

**Regime scale, from P1, notes to editors and body:** the cryptoasset financial-promotions regime has applied to *"all cryptoasset firms marketing to UK consumers, including firms based overseas"* **since October 2023**, and the FCA states *"the majority have reacted positively in complying with the new regime."* HTX is on the FCA Warning List.

## Explicit non-claims

1. **No allegation is asserted as fact.** Everything in §3 is the FCA's pleaded case, attributed to the FCA, untested at trial. HTX has filed no public response this corpus has seen.
2. **No named person is a defendant here.** Defendants 2–5 are unidentified classes.
3. **No prediction about settlement**, its likelihood, or the case's outcome.
4. **No claim that the stay pauses the whole case** — it is scoped to the First Defendant.
5. **No causal link between the UK proceedings and HTX's absence from ESMA's registers.** §4.
6. **No claim about the contents of the 4 February, 31 March or 25 June orders.** Only the 24 August order was fetched and read. The others are listed because P2 lists them.
7. **No claim that this is the first marketing-side enforcement action anywhere** — the FCA's own claim, quoted in §3, is narrower: the first time *the FCA* has taken enforcement action against a crypto firm for illegally marketing to UK consumers.
8. **This is not an EU/NCA action and does not touch watch (b).** Watch (b) tracks a first named post-deadline marketing-side action by an EU national competent authority under MiCA. **This is the FCA, under s.21 FSMA, on a claim issued eight months before the MiCA deadline.** The EU enforcement null recorded since day 46 is **unaffected and still stands.**
