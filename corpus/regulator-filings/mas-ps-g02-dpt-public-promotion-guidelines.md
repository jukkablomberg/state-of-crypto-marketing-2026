## Why this matters to the report — MAS regulates marketing *reach*; MiCA regulates marketing *content*

This is the sharpest jurisdictional contrast in the corpus, and it reframes the report's central post-deadline finding.

**Under MiCA**, marketing communications must be fair, clear, not misleading, identifiable as marketing, and consistent with the white paper. The regime governs *what you may say*. It presumes you may advertise.

**Under PS-G02, the presumption is inverted.** MAS's expectation is that DPT service providers **should not promote their DPT services to the general public in Singapore at all** — irrespective of how fairly, clearly or non-misleadingly they do it. The channel is closed, not policed.

**Consequence for Theme 4, and it is a real one:** the corpus has spent 36 days documenting the absence of any named EU marketing-*conduct* case. That absence is a finding about a regime that has to *adjudicate* marketing. In Singapore the equivalent question largely does not arise, because the general-public channel is barred *ex ante*. **Two regulators, two philosophies: prohibit the reach, or adjudicate the content.** The EU chose adjudication and has not yet adjudicated. That is a much better framing of the null than "regulators are slow," and it is now anchored to both primaries.

## Verbatim extraction (marketing-relevant clauses)

**§1.3 — the expectation, stated as such:**

> "MAS stresses that DPT service providers should conduct themselves with the understanding that trading of DPTs is not suitable for the general public. These Guidelines set out MAS' expectation that DPT service providers should not promote their DPT services to the general public in Singapore."

**§2.1 — the channel ban, enumerated:**

> "DPT service providers should not portray the trading of DPTs in a manner that trivialises the high risks of trading in DPTs, and should not promote their DPT services in public areas in Singapore or through any other media directed at the general public in Singapore. This includes placing of any form of advertisements or promotional materials in public areas such as Singapore public transport, public transport venues, broadcast media or periodical publications, third party websites, social media platforms, public events or roadshows."

**§2.2 — THE CARVE-OUT, and it is the load-bearing clause for this corpus:**

> "DPT service providers may promote their services on their own corporate website, mobile applications, or official social media accounts, but must not trivialise the risks of trading in DPTs in a manner that is inconsistent with or contradicts the risk disclosures under the PS Act."

**§2.3 — influencers and third parties, barred by name:**

> "DPT service providers should also not engage third parties, such as social media influencers or third-party websites, to promote their DPT services to the general public in Singapore. This includes joint promotional campaigns to solicit new customers."

**§3.1 — physical access treated as promotion:**

> "Providing in-person access to DPT services in public areas through the use of automated teller machines ('ATMs') is a form of promotion of DPT services to the public. … DPT service providers should not provide physical ATMs in public areas in Singapore to facilitate public access to their DPT services."

**§4.2 — derivatives positioning:**

> "DPT service providers should not promote PTDs to the public as a convenient unregulated alternative to trading in DPTs. DPT service providers should not mislead the public that PTDs are less risky than DPTs."

**Footnote 4 — social-media specificity:**

> "there should not be promotional banners or pop-ad advertisements on social media platforms to general public or specific consumer segment in Singapore."

## Cross-reference to the campaign corpus — stated as facts, verdict REFUSED

On **2026-08-05** this corpus captured, live, `okx.com/en-sg/learn/mica-deposit-bonus-campaign` under an **"OKX Singapore"** masthead, serving a deposit-bonus promotion (`../marketing-campaigns/promotional-teardown-checkpoint-2026-08-05.md`).

Both facts are now in the corpus. **The corpus does not draw the inference, and Phase 2 must not either without legal review**, for three reasons stated here so a later run does not "discover" a violation that is not established:

1. **§2.2 expressly permits promotion on a firm's own corporate website.** An `okx.com/.../learn/` page is, on its face, inside the carve-out.
2. **PS-G02 applies to "DPT service providers" as defined** — licensees, banks, other FIs providing DPT services in Singapore, and transitional-exemption entities. Whether the entity serving that locale is within scope is **not established by this corpus**.
3. **The §2.1/§2.2 test that could bite — "trivialises the high risks"** — is a judgement about a specific creative against specific risk disclosures. That is an adjudication, and this corpus does not adjudicate.

**What may honestly be said:** the corpus holds a dated capture of a Singapore-locale acquisition promotion by a Tier-1 firm, and it holds the MAS instrument that governs promotion in that jurisdiction, including a carve-out that plausibly covers it. **That is a Theme-4 exposure-surface datum, not a finding of breach.**
