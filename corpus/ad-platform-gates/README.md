# corpus/ad-platform-gates/ — a seventh source class, created 2026-08-08

> **THIS DIRECTORY DOES NOT CONTAIN REGULATOR ACTIONS. NOTHING IN IT IS ENFORCEMENT.**
> It contains dated, first-party **advertising-platform policies** that condition access to paid distribution on a firm's regulatory authorisation status.

## Why it exists

`methodology.md` defines six source classes. An ad platform's licensing gate fits none of them:

- **Not class 3 (regulator filings).** Google is not a regulator. It has no statutory power, its rule is contractual, and its remedy is account suspension, not sanction. The 2026-07-27 run recorded this correctly and therefore *excluded* the item from class 3.
- **Not class 2 (agency claims), not class 5 (layoffs), not class 1 (postings).**

The consequence of having no home was that a **fully public, fully citable, dated mechanism sat in the corpus as a parenthetical inside watch item (m) for twelve days** — "(m) Ad-platform gating — unchanged (Google France, 2026-07-01)" — carried from 2026-07-27 to 2026-08-07 without a single primary URL being fetched. When the primaries were finally read on 2026-08-08 they contained **four facts the corpus did not hold**, including two additional Member-State deadlines and an EEA extension in force this month.

This is watch item **(ee)** generalised: *a fact carried as a watch note is a fact not being used as evidence.*

## What qualifies for this directory

A dated, first-party policy document from an advertising or distribution platform (Google Ads, Meta, X, TikTok, Apple App Store, Google Play, ad networks) that:

1. conditions the ability to advertise or distribute on a **regulatory authorisation status**, and
2. carries a **stated effective date**, and
3. is captured from the platform's **own** documentation, not from trade press.

## The rule that must survive into Phase 2

**Never print a platform gate as regulator enforcement, and never let the two share a sentence without the distinction being explicit.**

The gate answers *who is permitted to buy distribution*. It says nothing about whether a given advertisement is fair, clear and not misleading under MiCA Article 7 / Article 66. **It gates the advertiser, not the ad.** That gap — a fully-enforced credential gate sitting on top of an entirely unenforced conduct standard — is the report's Theme-1 gate-stack argument in one sentence, and it is only available to the report if the two things are kept apart.

## Known gaps (absence = data)

Never swept at source: **Meta**, **X**, **TikTok**, **Apple App Store**, **Google Play**, **Reddit**, **LinkedIn**. Prior runs surfaced references to Meta and X crypto-ad policy changes in *search results* and correctly declined to enter them unfetched. They remain unfetched. The corpus therefore holds **one platform of at least seven**, and must not describe the ad-platform gate as characterised until more are read.
