# Google Ads' MiCA CASP advertising gate — EU-27 and EEA

**Captured:** 2026-08-08 (day 38 post-deadline)
**Source class:** 7 — ad-platform gate. **NOT class 3. Not a regulator action. Not enforcement.**
**Capture method:** three Google Advertising Policies Help pages fetched first-party, HTTP 200 each, no relay, no secondary.
**capture_ai_disclosure:** none — first-party platform documentation.

---

## Why this file exists, stated before the evidence

For **thirty-eight days** this corpus has recorded that no EU national competent authority has taken a named marketing-side enforcement action against a tracked-cohort CASP since the 1 July transitional deadline. On 2026-08-06 it established that the **AMF deliberately did not set a shutdown deadline** (`../regulator-filings/eu-nca-post-deadline-scam-warnings-and-amf-forbearance-2026-08-06.md`). On 2026-08-07 it established that **ESMA's own sanctions page excludes CASPs from its direct sanctioning perimeter** (`../regulator-filings/esma-sanctions-perimeter-casp-absence-2026-08-07.md`).

The obvious reader objection to a null is *"so nothing happened."*

**Something happened.** On **2026-07-01 — the day after France's MiCA transitional period expired — an advertising platform withdrew paid distribution from every French crypto firm that had not converted its AMF DASP registration into a MiCA CASP authorisation.** It did so on schedule, it had done the same thing in Finland thirteen months earlier and in Germany seven months earlier, and it extended the same rule to the remaining EEA states this month.

**The marketing-side consequence of MiCA authorisation status in the EEA is, on the present evidence, being administered on time by a private platform and not at all by the authorities that own the perimeter.** That sentence is the reason this file exists, and every element of it is sourced below.

## Source 1 — the EU baseline

**URL:** https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16089943
**Title:** *Updates to Cryptocurrency Advertising Policy in the EU (April 2025)*
**Posted:** March 24, 2025. **Effective:** April 23, 2025.

### Scope, verbatim

> "This policy applies to the following EU countries: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxemburg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, and Sweden"

Twenty-seven states, enumerated. **Every EU jurisdiction in this report's frame is inside the gate.**

### Requirements, verbatim

> "To advertise Cryptocurrency Exchanges and Software Wallets in the EU, advertisers must:
> - Be licensed as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider (CASP) under the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation by a relevant national competent authority.
> - Comply with all other local legal requirements, including any national-level restrictions or requirements beyond MiCA.
> - Be certified by Google."

Note the second bullet: the platform explicitly incorporates **national-level requirements beyond MiCA** into its own eligibility test. It is not merely mirroring MiCA; it is stacking national gold-plating on top of it.

### The transitional schedule — the fact the corpus did not hold

> "In Finland, France, and Germany, where Google currently accepts country-based licenses for advertising Cryptocurrency Exchanges and Software Wallets, these licenses will be recognized during the transitional periods established by each Member State **in accordance with Article 143(3) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (MiCA)**. Specifically:
> - **Finland:** Until June 30, 2025.
> - **France:** Until June 30, 2026.
> - **Germany:** Until December 30, 2025."

> "**Important:** After these transition periods, only MiCA licenses will be accepted for advertising crypto services in the EU."

**This is a private advertising platform keying its commercial eligibility rules to a named article of MiCA, Member State by Member State, with three distinct dates.** The corpus held one of those three dates and did not know the other two existed.

### Enforcement mechanism, verbatim

> "Violations of this policy will not lead to immediate account suspension without prior warning. A warning will be issued, at least 7 days, before any suspension of your account."

**A named remedy (account suspension) with a named procedural protection (≥7 days' notice).** This corpus does not hold a marketing-side remedy with a stated timeline from any regulator source in the post-deadline window. It now holds one from a platform.

## Source 2 — France, executed on the deadline

**URL:** https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17218519
**Title:** *Updates to Cryptocurrency Advertising Policy in France (July 2026)*
**Posted:** July 1, 2026. **Effective:** July 1, 2026.

Full operative text, verbatim:

> "Starting July 1, 2026, Google will update its Cryptocurrencies and related products policy in France. We will no longer accept registration from the AMF's Digital Asset Service Provider (DASP) for advertising cryptocurrency exchanges and wallets. Moving forward, all advertisers promoting these services must be authorized as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider (CASP) under the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation. This update follows the expiration of France's transitional period outlined in the original Updates to Cryptocurrency Advertising Policy in the EU (April 2025) change log."

### Read this against the corpus's own AMF finding

| | AMF (national competent authority) | Google (advertising platform) |
|---|---|---|
| French transitional period ends | 2026-06-30 | 2026-06-30 |
| Set a shutdown deadline for non-converted firms? | **No — deliberately did not** (08-06 capture) | **Yes — 2026-07-01** |
| Action taken on/after the deadline | No named marketing-side action, 38 days | DASP registration withdrawn as an advertising credential, day 1 |
| Remedy | — | Account suspension, ≥7 days' warning |

**The regulator that owns the French perimeter declined to set a date. The platform set one, published it in advance in April 2025, and met it.**

## Source 3 — the EEA completion, in force this month

**URL:** https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17264747
**Title:** *Update to Cryptocurrencies and related products policy (August 2026)*
**Posted:** July 22, 2026. **Effective:** August 2026 (month stated; no day given on the page — **do not invent one**).

Verbatim:

> "In August 2026, Google will be updating the Cryptocurrencies and related products policies to allow the promotion of cryptocurrency exchanges, software wallets, and hardware wallets in the following European Economic Area (EEA) countries: Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway."

> "In order to advertise cryptocurrency exchanges, software wallets, and hardware wallets in the EEA, advertisers must:
> - Be licensed as a Crypto-Asset Service Provider (CASP) under the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation by a relevant national competent authority.
> - Comply with all other local legal requirements, including any national-level restrictions or requirements beyond MiCA.
> - Be certified by Google."

> "Violations of this policy will not lead to immediate account suspension without prior warning. A warning will be issued, at least 7 days, prior to any suspension of your account."

**Note the direction of travel, because it cuts against the lazy reading.** This change is **permissive** — it *opens* three markets that were previously closed to crypto advertising entirely. The gate is not being tightened; it is being **extended**. Google is expanding the territory in which MiCA authorisation is the price of admission. EU-27 + IS/LI/NO = the EEA, complete.

## What is claimed, and what is NOT

**CLAIMED, on the text above:**
1. Google Ads requires MiCA CASP authorisation to advertise crypto exchanges and software wallets across the EU-27, and from August 2026 across the full EEA.
2. Its rule is keyed explicitly to MiCA Article 143(3) transitional periods, per Member State, with published dates.
3. It has executed three such deadlines — Finland 2025-06-30, Germany 2025-12-30, France 2026-06-30/07-01.
4. Its stated remedy is account suspension with at least 7 days' prior warning.
5. The August 2026 EEA change is an expansion of permitted territory, not a restriction.

**NOT CLAIMED, and must not be inferred:**
- **That this is enforcement.** It is a contractual eligibility rule. Google is not a regulator, has no statutory power, and its remedy is commercial.
- **That any specific firm lost advertising access.** No firm-level effect has been observed. The corpus has fetched the *policy*, not any *application* of it. **A gate is not a casualty list.**
- **That the gate is effective.** Whether Google enforces its own rule against non-authorised advertisers is entirely unmeasured here. **The corpus has documented, repeatedly and at Kraken, OKX, Bitpanda and BitMart, that published promotional rules and live promotional surfaces routinely diverge** (`../marketing-campaigns/promotional-teardown-checkpoint-2026-08-02.md`). Google's own estate deserves the identical suspicion. **A stated policy is a claim, not a measurement.**
- **That other platforms behave the same way.** Six other major distribution surfaces are unswept. See the directory README's gaps section.
- **That this addresses marketing conduct.** It does not. **It gates the advertiser, not the ad.** MiCA Article 7 and Article 66 require marketing communications to be fair, clear and not misleading and identifiable as marketing. Nothing in any of the three pages tests that. An authorised CASP running a materially misleading advertisement passes this gate without friction.

## The finding this supports, stated carefully

The report's day-38 null should never be printed as "silence." It should be printed as a **division of labour that no one designed**:

> The credential is gated. The conduct is not. The gate is operated by a platform on a published schedule; the conduct standard is owned by authorities that have taken no named marketing-side action in the thirty-eight days since the deadline, one of which deliberately set no deadline at all, and the EU-level authority among them has no direct sanctioning power over CASPs whatsoever.

Every clause of that sentence is anchored: this file (gate), `../regulator-filings/eu-nca-post-deadline-scam-warnings-and-amf-forbearance-2026-08-06.md` (no deadline), `../regulator-filings/esma-sanctions-perimeter-casp-absence-2026-08-07.md` (no perimeter).

It also converges with the corpus's own 08-06 reading that **"the licence has become the marketing claim, and is therefore now the thing being counterfeited"** — the AFM warning that fraudsters target consumers *searching for a licensed alternative*, and scammers deploying ESMA's own logo. If authorisation is the price of paid distribution across the whole EEA, authorisation becomes the highest-value claim in EU crypto marketing, which is precisely why it is worth forging.
