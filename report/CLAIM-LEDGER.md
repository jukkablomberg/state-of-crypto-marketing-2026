# Claim ledger — State of Crypto Marketing 2026 (v8 build 2026-09-15; v9 note 2026-09-16)

**v9 note.** v9 takes v7's text as the baseline at the author's request. Four v7 claims that v8 had narrowed are therefore back in print, as the author's interpretation (Type I), not as observations: the cover thesis "nobody will say who runs marketing"; "AI is not shrinking crypto marketing teams. The AI labs are hiring them" (Chapter 2, Our reading); "Marketing is the only function in crypto that competes for budget without a published number … that is why compliance wins the argument" (Chapter 4, Our reading); and "Eighteen of 27 have never put a number on their marketing" (the bounded-search finding A10, printed in its short form). The evidence rows below are unchanged; the "Removed or narrowed" table at the end records the v8 reasoning so a reader can see both positions. Three other v9 changes: the 2-vs-25 vacancy count (A8) is no longer printed — the largest exchanges' careers boards cannot be read, so the report says only that the boards it can read show few roles at the 27 and many at the AI labs; the agency panel prints as 17 (one agency that claimed none of the 27 omitted; no count moves), and Chapter 1 carries the dated Google-policy account (A6) instead of the v7 slogan.

One row per cover claim, executive-brief statistic, exhibit headline, bold conclusion and chapter takeaway. **Type**: O = observation (what the public record shows on a stated date), I = interpretation (ours, marked "Our reading" in the report), R = recommendation (labelled). **Src** = canonical source number `C-nn` as listed in `build/tables.py` (`CITES`, stable across versions); Appendix E of the PDF renumbers sources by first citation, and `BUILD.md` prints the mapping. Event date = when the thing happened; observed = when we read it. Anything that could not be supported within the timebox was narrowed or removed (see the "Removed" table at the end).
