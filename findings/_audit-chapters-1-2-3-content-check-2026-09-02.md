**Why the wording cannot be certified.** On a first pass the retrieval instrument substituted an entirely different passage when asked for "sterile brand." On a targeted second pass it affirmed as PRESENT **two mutually exclusive strings** — both *"And I think there's really strong gravitational pull towards being beige where I'm sitting"* and *"And there's really strong gravitational pull towards being beige where I'm sitting."* Both cannot be on the page. **An instrument that affirms a string and its variant cannot establish a verbatim quotation**, and this audit will not record one on its say-so.

**The unresolved discrepancy, recorded rather than asserted.** The chapter prints the beige passage as a single flowing quotation: *"…they're too beige, right? And there's really strong gravitational pull…"* The transcript appears to render two sentences, the second beginning **"And I think there's really strong gravitational pull…"**, and the first preceded by **"And I won't get into it, but…"**. If so, the chapter has elided material mid-quotation without ellipses and dropped "I think" — converting a hedged statement into a flat one. **This audit could not settle it, and that is precisely the point:** a quotation whose wording cannot be settled must not run as a quotation.

**Recommendation — paraphrase, at all three sites (Ch. 2, Ch. 3 and Ch. 5).** The *substance* is independently anchored and safe: Acast's own episode description says the episode covers **"how to build financial brands that don't feel sterile"** in **"an industry as regulated as fintech."** That is a publisher-issued sentence supporting the report's actual argument. Proposed replacements are in §5.

**The `[VERIFY]` tags asking for an audio check cannot be closed by any text source.** Either someone listens to the 32-minute episode before publication, or the quotations come out. There is no third option.
