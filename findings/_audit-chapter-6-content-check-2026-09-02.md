## §2 — The contradictions, in full, with proposed replacement wording

### C1 — The chapter mis-classifies three percentages as "outlet figures" when the outlet attributes them to the firm's own announcement

**This is the most consequential structural finding, because it recurs across three rows and cuts against the chapter's own stated rule.**

The chapter's rule (line 29) is: *"only figures the firm itself stated appear in it. **Outlet arithmetic, anonymous sourcing and derived headcounts are excluded** and listed beneath."* Crypto.com's −12%, BitGo's −15% and Uphold's −17%/85 are **none of those three things**. They are figures the capturing outlet attributes to the firm's own announcement, without wrapping them in quotation marks:

- **"Crypto.com co-founder and CEO Kris Marszalek announced Thursday the exchange is reducing its workforce by approximately 12%."**
- **"BitGo CEO Mike Belshe announced that the company is reducing its workforce by 15%."**
- **"Crypto trading platform Uphold said it cut roughly 17% of its global workforce…"**

Under the corpus's own A–E ladder this is **Grade B — firm-stated, relayed** — the same grade Coinbase's −14% and Robinhood's −10% carry, both of which the chapter *does* print in the figure column. The distinction the corpus has actually drawn is between a number **inside quotation marks** and a number **attributed in reported speech** — which is a distinction about typography, not about evidence.

**Sentence to change (line 67):**
> ~~"Crypto.com's ~180 is The Block's arithmetic against a previously disclosed base, and the outlet says so; the −12% is its headline, with no firm quote behind the number."~~

**Proposed:**
> "Crypto.com's ~180 is The Block's arithmetic against a previously disclosed base, and the outlet says so. The −12% is not inside a firm quotation, but it is not the outlet's own arithmetic either — The Block attributes the announcement to the chief executive: *'CEO Kris Marszalek announced Thursday the exchange is reducing its workforce by approximately 12%.'* It is firm-stated relayed, and is printed as such."

**Sentence to change (line 67, later):**
> ~~"Kraken's ~150 and −5%, Uphold's 85 (which includes contractors), the Ethereum Foundation's 54 and BitGo's −15% are outlet figures with no firm quote."~~

**Proposed:**
> "Kraken's ~150 and −5% and the Ethereum Foundation's 54 sit in the outlets' own text with no firm attribution. Uphold's 85 (which includes contractors) and −17%, and BitGo's −15%, are different: no firm quotation carries them, but the capturing outlet attributes each to the firm's own statement — *'Uphold said it cut roughly 17% of its global workforce'*; *'BitGo CEO Mike Belshe announced that the company is reducing its workforce by 15%.'* They are firm-stated relayed, and the tracker's C grades on those two cells understate them."

**Table rows to change:** Crypto.com (line 37), BitGo (line 55) and Uphold (line 61) currently print *"— none"* in the **Firm-stated figure** column. Each should carry its percentage with a **(B — relayed)** marker, or the column heading should be narrowed to **"Figure in the firm's own quoted words."** *Recommendation: narrow the heading.* It is the smaller edit, it keeps every existing cell true, and it names the distinction the corpus is actually applying.

### C2 — MARA: the reason given for the bar is wrong, though the bar itself is right

**Sentence to change (line 75):**
> ~~"…while 'AI' and 'HPC' are the outlets' framing and the CEO's AI language attaches to a bitcoin sale one paragraph up the page."~~

The source sentence explicitly covers the layoff: **"CEO Fred Thiel has framed the bitcoin sales, debt reduction, *and now the workforce cuts* as part of a deliberate transition away from pure-play mining toward AI and high-performance computing infrastructure."** The chapter's claim that Thiel's AI language attaches only to a bitcoin sale is contradicted at source.

**The ruling survives on better grounds:** the AI/HPC words are the **outlet's characterisation of Thiel's framing**, not a quoted MARA statement. No MARA statement about the layoff — spokesperson or CEO — uses either word. The spokesperson's own layoff statement names *"an energy and digital infrastructure company."*

**Proposed:**
> "…while 'AI' and 'HPC' appear nowhere in any quoted MARA statement about the layoff.
