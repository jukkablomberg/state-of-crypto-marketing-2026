## Shift flagged this month

The **sport-sponsorship reset** thesis — first coded in `../corpus/operator-statements/sport-sponsorship-reset-2026-05.md` from the operator/P&L side — gained a **regulatory-pressure leg** in June 2026:

- **Sell-side (already documented, May):** exchanges exiting sport deals on conversion-economics (Bybit/Red Bull) and counterparty-risk (Zondacrypto/Tour de Suisse) grounds; CMO seat shifting from brand-deal-maker to P&L-defender.
- **Buy-side (new this month, June):** the **FCA publicly warned the host properties themselves** — Premier League / football clubs — that taking money from unauthorised crypto sponsors is a financial-promotion / money-laundering / reputational exposure, contacted clubs, and signalled enforcement (2026-06-02/03; see `../corpus/regulator-filings/fca-premier-league-sponsorship-warning-2026-06.md`).

**Synthesis implication:** the crypto sport-sponsorship line item is now squeezed from both ends simultaneously — the buyer (exchange) is cutting it on economics, and the regulator is pressuring the seller (club) on legality. For the report's Theme 4 read, this is a clean example of the regulator policing the *channel/placement*, not just the firm's owned marketing copy — i.e., "what is publicly visible" now includes shirt/stadium inventory, not only landing pages.

## Update — week of June 21–26: the second regulatory leg (entity-permission / solicitation)

June 2026 now shows the regulator policing crypto marketing at **two distinct layers in the same month**:

- **Placement / channel layer (early June):** FCA → football clubs, on sponsorship inventory (above).
- **Entity-permission / solicitation layer (late June, new):** as the **MiCA transitional period ends 1 July 2026**, **ESMA's Public Statement of 23 June 2026** (ref. ESMA75-113276571-1710) instructs unauthorised CASPs to wind down, and names **"cease marketing activities and solicitation" as the first operative obligation** — ahead of winding down the service itself. It extends to extraterritorial and B2B solicitation (narrow reverse-solicitation carve-out only), requires repeated client wind-down communications, and signals coordinated NCA action after the deadline. See `../corpus/regulator-filings/esma-mica-transitional-period-end-2026-06.md`.

**Synthesis implication (Theme 4 + Theme 1):** for a tracked firm that misses MiCA authorisation, the *first publicly observable act* of an orderly wind-down is marketing going dark. That makes the methodology's "what is publicly visible" filter directly load-bearing on the enforcement read — marketing-channel silence is now itself an enforcement-state signal, not just an absence-of-signal data point. Worth a Theme-4 sub-section: *"marketing cessation as the leading public indicator of CASP authorisation failure."* Watch the **1-July deadline week** for the first named-firm instance (a significant unauthorised cross-border CASP visibly pulling EU marketing).
